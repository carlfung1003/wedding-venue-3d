# CONTACT — what the reference sheets say (KAN-207, 2026-09-16)

Thirteen orthographic sheets, one per asset, generated by `gen_art.py` (entries of
kind `refsheet` in `art_manifest.json`) from 1–3 crops of the planner's renders,
laid out as ONE ROW of four panels: front · left side · back · three-quarter, on a
shared ground line. Each sheet has a `<name>.measure.json` beside it (the vendored
`refsheet-to-3d` measure: per-panel height, width÷height, width profile) and was
cross-checked with the skill's own `measure_sheet.py` — same numbers.

**Read this, not the images.** The sheets settle *design* (silhouette, proportion,
material language, the details the spec does not say); `ASSET_SPEC.md` settles the
metres. Where the two disagree the spec wins and the disagreement is written here so
nobody re-derives it. The sheets are gitignored; only this file travels.

Conventions: `H` is the front panel's full height (the asset's real height from the
spec, stated per row), and every fraction is a fraction of `H` unless it says
otherwise. "Spread" is the height disagreement between the three orthographic panels
(front/side/back); under 3 % the panels share a scale and can be measured together.
The three-quarter panel is never measured — it is the detail authority only.

⚠ Three measurement traps hit on these sheets, already corrected in the numbers below:
1. thick panel dividers on `coconut_rack`, `arch_bead_chandelier` (side) and `dj_booth`
   (side) read as the object's extent in the raw `.measure.json` (width/height comes
   out as exactly the panel width, e.g. 0.502 × 1015 px); the numbers here come from
   a 14 px panel inset. Treat any `width_over_height` that equals `panel_width ÷ h`
   as an artefact;
2. white objects on a white ground (the dessert counter body) leave only their outline,
   which the 3 px erosion removes — the counter measures as empty; its numbers are the
   spec's;
3. a wide bounding box is not the object: the `dj_booth` side panel is 6 % taller than
   the front because of the laptop lid, not a scale error.
