# Clubhouse entrance + parking — arrival brief

**Master video (gitignored, not in repo):** `reference/video/clubhouse-entrance-parking.mp4`
**Committed source for this brief:** `reference/video/clubhouse-entrance-parking-frames/f_001.jpg`…`f_032.jpg` (32 frames, 1 fps, time order), read frame-by-frame for this document.

**Scope note, stated up front so nobody re-derives it by re-watching:** the video is a single continuous handheld walk. Only **frames 1–2** show the parking apron / building exterior from a distance. **Frames 3–13** are the walk up to and onto the entrance stair. **Frames 14–32 — the majority of the clip — are INTERIOR**: the camera passes through the entrance doors into the lounge/atrium and pans across it, looking back out through floor-to-ceiling folding glass at a courtyard with a pool and villa balconies on both sides. That interior material is **out of scope for this brief** (this brief is the arrival/parking approach only) but is noted briefly in §1 because it confirms the "atrium is the corridor" model CLAUDE.md already documents, and a future interior-focused brief should use frames 14–32 as its source.

## 1 · What the video shows, in camera order

### Frames 1–2 — parking apron + building exterior, seen from a distance (the ONLY exterior-parking-lot frames in the clip)

- The clubhouse presents from this angle as a **dark, weathered box**: vertical-batten cladding with a streaked, oxidised metal/rusted-steel look (browns, near-blacks, occasional orange bleed) — not a stucco surface, and not the "white stucco volumes / copper-bronze fascia" palette `campus.js`'s header comment documents for the rest of the clubhouse. This may simply be the entry pavilion getting a deliberately contrasting material (plausible resort design move) — but as filmed, it reads dark and industrial-modern, not white and Mediterranean.
- **Roofline:** a dramatic **mono-pitch / folded-plate roof** in the same dark metal, sloping down from a tall ridge to a low eave that cantilevers out over the entry as a flat canopy. Sharp, angular, almost origami-like fold at the corner (frame 1).
- **Signage:** a light/white horizontal plaque mounted flush on the dark cladding, dark characters, two lines (a larger line above a smaller subtitle line). **Illegible at this resolution** — contextually almost certainly reads 隐逸居, but do not commit exact glyphs without a higher-res source photo.
- **Foreground planting (this angle):** a large multi-trunk **frangipani/plumeria tree** (broad spreading canopy, ~3.5–4 m tall, sparse pink-flowered) anchors the corner where the building turns; below it a dense clipped hedge mixed with dark maroon **cordyline/ti-plant** clumps and small red flowers (ixora-type) at the base — a layered, colourful foundation bed, not a plain green hedge.
- **Paving, this area:** in frame 1's near-camera foreground, a **darker grey rectangular-paver / stone-sett surface** (herringbone- or basket-weave-look joints, NOT a smooth poured slab) with a partial **tan/sand-toned circular paving inlay** bottom-frame, and partial **white painted stall stripes** at the right edge — i.e., the apron itself is unit pavers, and only the stall markings are painted lines, not the whole surface being flat asphalt.
- A raised kerb/planter edge (light grey, ~150–200 mm) separates this paved apron from the planting bed described above.
- Frame 2 (a few steps further, same standing position, closer): a low dark structure with **two vertical fluted gold/brass cylindrical elements** is visible at the far left edge of frame — likely a portico post or shelter, cut off by the frame edge; not enough of it is visible to model with confidence. Note it as an unresolved detail rather than invent it.
- **No cars are actually visible in these two frames** — the "parking lot" is inferred from the paved apron + stall-stripe fragment only. The video does not give a wide, uninterrupted view of the stall layout, row count, or aisle width. Treat "12 cars in a row" (the current build's assumption) as **unverified by this video** — neither confirmed nor contradicted.

### Frames 3–13 — the walk from apron to the entrance stair

- **Approach path:** a light grey, matte, large-format (~600 mm square, by eye) concrete/stone paving, laid as a straight, wide walkway/forecourt leading dead-on to the entrance. Scale anchor: relative to the flanking potted plants (~0.6 m pot diameter) the forecourt reads roughly 5–6 pot-widths wide, so **on the order of 5–7 m across** — wide enough for two-way pedestrian flow, not a footpath.
- **Flanking planting (symmetrical both sides, the whole approach):** mature multi-trunk **frangipani/plumeria** trees at both left and right (matching the ones in frames 1–2), underplanted with a **clipped green hedge** (~0.5 m tall) interspersed with dark-red **cordyline** clumps and small red/orange flowers at the hedge foot. The bed is edged with a stone/concrete kerb raised ~150–200 mm above the forecourt paving, running the full length of the approach on both sides.
- **Entry stair:** a wide flight of **dark, near-black polished stone** (granite or engineered stone, glossy/reflective finish) steps — by eye **5–6 risers**, standard-feeling proportions (scale anchor: comparable to a typical 150–170 mm riser / 300–350 mm tread, giving a total rise on the order of **0.9–1.0 m** from forecourt to entry landing — a modest podium step-up, not a grand monumental stair). The stair spans the full width of the recessed entry bay — by eye, **roughly 8–10 m wide** (anchor: about the width of three door-leaf bays plus margins) — flanked on both sides by raised stone side-plinths at landing height.
- **Symmetric potted plants on the landing:** two large **matte-white bowl planters** (~0.5–0.6 m dia.) right at the base of the steps, each holding a slender variegated grass-leaved small tree (dracaena/"song of India" type or similar) — a different species from the frangipani, positioned as the immediate door-flanking accent.
- **Facade at the entry bay:** dark anodised metal/glass curtain wall in **alternating horizontal bands** — dark charcoal panel bands alternating with lighter taupe-grey stone-look bands — forming two flanking piers either side of the recessed door opening.
- **Wall sconces:** one pair of vertical **fluted brass/gold cylindrical lantern sconces**, mounted on the dark piers immediately flanking the doors (clear in frames 13, 17, 18) — a decorative, warm-metal accent against the otherwise cool/dark facade.
- **Doors:** dark-framed glass double doors (reads as an automatic slider, multi-leaf) recessed under a **deep flat canopy**; the canopy's underside is a **slatted screen ceiling** (wood-look or metal slats) with a row of small recessed downlights visible in frames 7–9.
- **No gate, no boom barrier, no freestanding portal/monument** ahead of the building — the "entrance" is the building facade itself, met head-on by the paved approach. **No ramp is visible** anywhere in frame — stairs only.
- **No water feature** (fountain, pond, reflecting pool) anywhere in the forecourt/approach frames.

### Frames 14–32 — interior (out of scope; noted for completeness)

Camera passes through the entry doors into a lounge with cream low-back sofas facing dark wood coffee tables, on a blue-grey rug with a pale wave/ripple line motif, dark timber ceiling/mullions, floor-to-ceiling **folding glass door-walls** (matches `campus.js`'s documented palette line "folding glass door-walls"), sheer curtains, and a transition from stone floor near the entry to wood flooring further in. Through the glass, an outdoor courtyard/terrace with wicker dining furniture, a pergola-like structure, and villa-style buildings with balconies on both left and right flanks the space — visually consistent with CLAUDE.md's existing description of the atrium as a corridor that guest villas hang off of. No new geometry decisions are made from this footage in this brief.

## 2 · Spatial relationships

- The parking apron (frames 1–2) sits **immediately beside** the pedestrian approach — the camera walks from one to the other in a single continuous, level, dry walk (frames 2→3) with **no bridge, ramp, or water crossing** anywhere in the transition.
- The entrance stair/forecourt is a **short, direct, dead-ahead walk** from the parking-adjacent standing point — by the frame count (roughly 10–11 seconds/frames from the parking view to reaching the foot of the stair), this reads as a **short walk, well under 30–40 m**, not a long processional approach.
- The clubhouse entrance facade is met **head-on**: the pedestrian route runs perpendicular into the building face, stair centred on the door bay, symmetrical planting either side of that centreline.
- The building's massing continues to the left and right beyond the entry bay (visible in frames 1, 3, 4 as the dark cladding running off both edges of frame), consistent with the clubhouse being one larger volume and this being a single entry point on one face of it, not a standalone portal structure.
- Once inside, the atrium reads as a corridor with a courtyard/pool directly ahead and villa wings visible left and right through the far glass wall — i.e., **parking → forecourt/stair → entry doors → atrium corridor → courtyard**, all roughly on one axis, matching CLAUDE.md's "atrium is like a hotel hallway" model.

## 3 · Deltas vs current build

Current build reference: `js/campus.js` `buildRoad()` (lines ~2981–3053, "9 · arrival road, parking apron, lamp posts, drive spur to the clubhouse") and its `FC` forecourt block (lines ~3006–3017). Palette baseline: `campus.js` header comment, "white stucco volumes · flat cantilevered roofs with copper/bronze fascia · dark mahogany slats · cream marble …".

1. **No entrance structure exists at the forecourt at all** — the build has a paver circle (`CircleGeometry` r 6.2), a turf island (r 2.4), one `box()` stone block (5.2×5.2×.22), and a single generic `hedgeI` blob at `(FC.x, .95, FC.z)`. The video shows a full building facade there: dark folded-metal roof, banded curtain-wall piers, a 5–6-riser stone stair, brass sconces, and glass entry doors. Nothing resembling a door, a stair, or a facade is built at the forecourt today.
2. **Parking surface material mismatch** — build: `MAT.park`, a flat canvas-texture material (`texPark()`) painted with straight white stripes every 64 px on a uniform dark-grey noise field, i.e. one continuous slab. Video: a **jointed stone/paver sett surface** (herringbone or basket-weave units), separate from a distinct tan circular paving inlay, with stall stripes as a secondary marking on top of the pavers, not the whole story.
3. **Planting at the arrival is a single generic blob vs. a layered, species-specific bed** — build: one `UNIT_BLOB` `hedgeI` instance, flat green (`0x2f5a2c`). Video: symmetrical **frangipani/plumeria trees** + clipped hedge + **cordyline** (dark red foliage) + small flowering accents (ixora-type), edged with a raised stone kerb, run the full length of the approach on both sides. None of frangipani, cordyline, or ixora exist as species in `nature.js` today (only coconut palm and bougainvillea are modelled there).
4. **No stair / grade change is modelled at the arrival** — build's forecourt, drive spur, and parking apron are all essentially flat (`y ≈ .04–.16`). Video shows a genuine ~0.9–1.0 m podium rise via a 5–6 riser stair between the forecourt paving and the entry landing.
5. **No entry doors, curtain-wall piers, sconces, or canopy are modelled anywhere near the arrival** — the nearest built asset is the generic `buildLounge()` interior (dressed elsewhere, per the file header "the lounge floor is deliberately left EMPTY"); there is no exterior door-and-facade asset that the forecourt walks up to.
6. **Vehicle-to-door continuity:** build's comments (`campus.js` ~2993–3005, CLAUDE.md ~809–813) establish that a **river now separates the parking/road from the clubhouse**, forcing guests to be "dropped inland and walk in." The video's continuous walk from the parking-adjacent point to the stair shows **no water crossing at all** — flat, dry pavement the whole way. This is not necessarily a contradiction (the filmed lot may be a different/closer lot than the one the build's river-crossing logic addresses, or the crossing may be off to one side, out of frame) — but it is a real discrepancy worth flagging rather than silently building past: **the build's mental model has a water gap between car and door; the filmed walk has none.**
7. **Palette contrast:** build's documented clubhouse palette is "white stucco … copper/bronze fascia … cream marble." The filmed entrance is dark, weathered, metal-clad, with a folded dark roof — the opposite read. Either this entry pavilion is an intentional dark accent against a white clubhouse (plausible, common in resort design) or the palette note needs revisiting for this face specifically. Flag, don't silently reconcile.
8. **Signage** — build's `buildSign()` / `SIGN_PILLAR` (referenced in the file header) is presumably a freestanding pillar; the video shows a **flush wall-mounted plaque** on the entry facade itself, not a freestanding sign structure. Different object entirely if the build's sign is meant to represent this same nameplate.
9. **No lighting fixtures resembling the sconces exist in this area** — build's arrival lighting is `poleI` lamp posts (3.1 m pole + lamp box) along the road and small `glowI` bollards along the spur; none of that matches the door-flanking brass fluted wall sconces or the canopy's recessed downlight row seen in the video.

## 4 · Priority read — the 3 deltas that matter most

CLAUDE.md's stated priority rule: *"Three heroes: the 隐逸居 clubhouse enclave, the Westin crescent, the water system... the road, the parking [are] context"* — but that rule is about the **generic resort road and resort-scale parking**, which are correctly deprioritised. This brief's entrance stair/facade/planting is different: it is the **clubhouse's own front door**, i.e. part of the 隐逸居 hero itself, since it's the first thing a guest walking in from the lot actually sees of the clubhouse. On that basis:

1. **Build the entrance facade + stair (delta #1 / #4 / #5 combined).** Right now a guest walking (or flying) up to the forecourt meets a hedge blob and a paver circle — nothing that reads as "arriving at 隐逸居." This is the single highest-value fix: a dark-clad recessed door bay, a 5–6 riser stone stair, and the canopy overhead, even simplified, immediately makes the arrival legible as arriving at the clubhouse hero rather than at an empty traffic circle.
2. **Fix the planting bed (delta #3).** The current single green blob reads as placeholder wherever a walker or flyover passes it, and planting is cheap (a few more instanced blobs + one new tree species) relative to the read it buys — frangipani + cordyline + kerb immediately signals "resort clubhouse entrance" rather than "generic hedge."
3. **Resolve the water-crossing discrepancy (delta #6).** This isn't a visual asset so much as a layout question: if the real venue's parking-to-door walk is dry and short, the current river-severs-parking-from-clubhouse logic may be staging the guest experience wrong at the single moment (arrival) most likely to be walked/flown deliberately. It's cheap to check against the site map / aerial before more geometry gets built on top of the current forecourt assumption, and expensive to unwind later if the forecourt gets developed further first.

Deltas #2 (paver material), #7 (palette contrast), #8 (signage), and #9 (sconces/lighting) are real but lower-value polish — worth doing once #1–#3 are settled, not before.
